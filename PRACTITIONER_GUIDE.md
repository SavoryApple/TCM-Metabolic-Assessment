# TCM Metabolic Assessment - Practitioner Guide

## Quick Reference for Clinical Use

### Assessment Process

1. **Patient Preparation**
   - Explain the purpose: holistic health evaluation through TCM lens
   - Clarify scoring system (0-3 scale)
   - Encourage honest, reflective responses
   - Consider recent health changes or events

2. **Conducting the Assessment**
   - Read each symptom clearly
   - Allow patient time to reflect
   - Clarify any unfamiliar terms
   - Note hesitations or strong reactions
   - Complete all 50 symptoms for comprehensive view

3. **Scoring Guidelines**
   - **0 (Never)**: Rarely or never experienced in past 3 months
   - **1 (Occasionally)**: 1-2 times per month, mild, doesn't affect daily life significantly
   - **2 (Frequently)**: 1-2 times per week, moderate intensity, noticeable impact
   - **3 (Constantly)**: Daily or near-daily, severe, significantly affects quality of life

### Interpretation Framework

#### Score Ranges by Element
- **0-5**: Balanced, minimal concern
- **6-10**: Mild imbalance, preventive care recommended
- **11-15**: Moderate imbalance, treatment indicated
- **16-20**: Significant imbalance, priority treatment
- **21+**: Severe imbalance, comprehensive intervention needed

#### Pattern Identification

**Excess Patterns** (High scores + specific symptom clusters):
- **Liver Qi Stagnation**: Irritability, sighing, rib pain, PMS
- **Heart Fire**: Anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, red face/tongue
- **Spleen Dampness**: Weight gain, bloating, loose stools, heavy limbs
- **Lung Heat**: Cough, yellow phlegm, thirst, skin inflammation
- **Kidney Yang Deficiency with Dampness**: Edema, cold, frequent urination, low back pain

**Deficiency Patterns** (Moderate scores + weakness symptoms):
- **Liver Blood Deficiency**: Dry eyes, brittle nails, pale face, scanty menses
- **Heart Qi/Blood Deficiency**: Palpitations, poor memory, pale tongue, fatigue
- **Spleen Qi Deficiency**: Fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, weak limbs
- **Lung Qi Deficiency**: Weak voice, spontaneous sweating, shortness of breath
- **Kidney Yin/Yang Deficiency**: Tinnitus, hair loss, bone issues, lack of willpower

### Element Interaction Patterns

#### Generating Cycle Applications
**When one element is deficient, consider tonifying its "mother":**
- Weak Fire → Tonify Wood (nourish Liver to support Heart)
- Weak Earth → Tonify Fire (strengthen Heart to support Spleen)
- Weak Metal → Tonify Earth (boost Spleen to support Lung)
- Weak Water → Tonify Metal (enhance Lung to support Kidney)
- Weak Wood → Tonify Water (fortify Kidney to support Liver)

#### Controlling Cycle Applications
**When one element is in excess, consider its controller:**
- Excess Wood → Sedate Wood, Tonify Metal
- Excess Fire → Sedate Fire, Tonify Water
- Excess Earth → Sedate Earth, Tonify Wood
- Excess Metal → Sedate Metal, Tonify Fire
- Excess Water → Sedate Water, Tonify Earth

#### Common Multi-Element Patterns
1. **Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Deficiency** (Wood overacting on Earth)
   - High Wood score + High Earth score
   - Treatment: Soothe Liver, Strengthen Spleen

2. **Heart and Kidney Not Harmonized** (Fire-Water disharmony)
   - High Fire + High Water scores
   - Treatment: Nourish Kidney Yin, Clear Heart Fire

3. **Lung and Kidney Deficiency** (Metal-Water both weak)
   - Moderate scores in both
   - Treatment: Tonify both, strengthen the root (Kidney)

### Treatment Strategy Development

#### Priority Setting
1. **Acute/Severe Symptoms**: Address immediately (score 3 symptoms)
2. **Dominant Element**: Focus on highest scoring element
3. **Root Cause**: Consider element relationships and underlying deficiencies
4. **Patient Concerns**: Incorporate chief complaints

#### Treatment Modalities by Element

**Wood (Liver/Gallbladder)**
- Acupuncture: LV3, LV8, GB34, GB20
- Herbs: Xiao Yao San (Free and Easy Wanderer), Long Dan Xie Gan Tang
- Lifestyle: Stress management, regular exercise, anger management
- Diet: Sour flavors in moderation, leafy greens, avoid alcohol

**Fire (Heart/Small Intestine)**
- Acupuncture: HT7, HT6, PC6, SI3
- Herbs: Tian Wang Bu Xin Dan, Huang Lian E Jiao Tang
- Lifestyle: Meditation, adequate sleep, reduce stimulants
- Diet: Bitter flavors, red foods, avoid excessive spicy/hot foods

**Earth (Spleen/Stomach)**
- Acupuncture: ST36, SP6, SP9, CV12
- Herbs: Si Jun Zi Tang, Xiang Sha Liu Jun Zi Tang
- Lifestyle: Regular meals, reduce worry, gentle exercise
- Diet: Sweet flavors (natural), warm cooked foods, avoid cold/raw

**Metal (Lung/Large Intestine)**
- Acupuncture: LU7, LU9, LI4, LI11
- Herbs: Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang, Yu Ping Feng San
- Lifestyle: Deep breathing exercises, letting go practices
- Diet: Pungent flavors, white foods, adequate hydration

**Water (Kidney/Bladder)**
- Acupuncture: KI3, KI7, BL23, BL52, GV4
- Herbs: Liu Wei Di Huang Wan, Jin Gui Shen Qi Wan
- Lifestyle: Adequate rest, avoid overexertion, cultivate courage
- Diet: Salty flavors in moderation, dark/black foods, bone broths

### Follow-Up Assessment

**Recommended Timeline:**
- Acute conditions: Weekly for 4-6 weeks
- Chronic conditions: Every 2-4 weeks initially
- Maintenance: Monthly or quarterly

**Tracking Progress:**
- Compare total scores by element
- Note which symptoms improved/worsened
- Assess shift in dominant elements
- Patient-reported quality of life changes

### Clinical Documentation Tips

1. **Initial Assessment**: Record all scores, note dominant elements, list 3-5 primary treatment goals
2. **Pattern Diagnosis**: Document TCM pattern diagnosis based on assessment + other diagnostic methods
3. **Treatment Plan**: Specify modalities, point selections, herbal formulas, lifestyle recommendations
4. **Follow-Up Notes**: Track score changes, symptom resolution, treatment modifications

### Red Flags and Referrals

**Consider medical referral if:**
- Chest pain with exertion or palpitations at rest
- Severe or sudden onset headaches
- Unexplained significant weight loss
- Blood in stool or urine
- Severe depression or suicidal ideation
- Symptoms suggesting emergency conditions

### Cultural Considerations

- Adapt language to patient's understanding of TCM
- Respect cultural health beliefs and practices
- Consider dietary restrictions and preferences
- Be sensitive to discussion of emotions in different cultures

### Integrative Approach

This assessment complements but doesn't replace:
- Pulse diagnosis (脉诊)
- Tongue diagnosis (舌诊)
- Palpation examination (按诊)
- Medical history and questioning (问诊)
- Visual observation (望诊)

Use as one component of comprehensive TCM assessment.

## Sample Clinical Case

**Patient Profile**: 42-year-old female
**Chief Complaint**: Fatigue, irritability, poor sleep

**Assessment Scores:**
- Wood: 18 (High)
- Fire: 14 (Moderate)
- Earth: 16 (High)
- Metal: 7 (Mild)
- Water: 9 (Mild)

**Pattern Diagnosis**: Liver Qi Stagnation with Spleen Qi Deficiency (Wood overacting on Earth)

**Treatment Strategy:**
1. **Primary Focus**: Soothe Liver Qi, Tonify Spleen Qi
2. **Acupuncture**: LV3, LV14, SP6, ST36, PC6, HT7
3. **Herbal Formula**: Xiao Yao San modified
4. **Lifestyle**: Stress reduction techniques, regular meals, gentle walking
5. **Diet**: Reduce coffee, increase warming cooked foods, more vegetables

**Expected Outcome**: Reduction in Wood and Earth scores within 4-6 weeks with consistent treatment
